Introduction to the paper 

        Nanotechnology is the science of incredibly small materials and devices which operate on the scale of nanometres, which is one billionth of a metre (https://www.sciencenewstoday.org/what-is-nanotechnology ). If human hair were cut up into a hundred-thousand pieces, each piece would still be larger than a nanometre. 
 In 2015, an international organisation composed of 193 member states, named United Nations, agreed upon 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs for short) to work towards for 2030 (https://www.un.org/en/about-us ). The purpose of these goals was to improve living & environmental quality globally, as well as recognising many problems affecting the globe go hand in hand e.g. poverty and lack of educations, conflict with food security. SDG progress reports are written every year, recording the developments made since the SDGs’ establishment. It is 2026 currently, so we are nearing the end of this Agenda’s agreed run time.
