Introduction to the paper 

        In 2015, an international organisation composed of 193 member states, named United Nations, agreed upon 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs for short) Agenda to work towards for 2030 (https://www.un.org/en/about-us ). 
        The purpose of these goals was to improve living & environmental quality globally, the approach of tackling 17 goals at once was done to acknowledge that many global issues go hand in hand e.g. armed conflicts and resource insecurity.  
        But before we continue, what even is Nanotechnology and how does it link to the SDGs? 
        Nanotechnology is the science of incredibly small materials and devices operating on the scale of nanometres, which is one billionth of a metre. To emphasise how small this is, if a strand of human hair was cut up into a hundred-thousand pieces, each piece would still be larger than a nanometre! (according to: https://www.sciencenewstoday.org/what-is-nanotechnology). 
        I believe this article is relevant, as it is currently 2026 meaning we are nearing the end of this Agenda’s agreed run time. 
        My purpose in writing this is to reduce part of the mystery surrounding the nature of nanotech and whether using it can ensure we achieve all SDG goals before the end of 2030. 
        The following article will be split into two sections, where I will explain how nanotechnology is used within a specific field. Within both sections, I will go on to talk about how the implementation of nanotech in that field is already helping people in some regions reach SDG goals and how a global implementation of the same technology would likely let us achieve that SDG worldwide. 
        At the end of this article, there will be a conclusion where I will go over the main talking points and conclude whether nanotech is extremely useful, not helpful at all, or anything in between.
